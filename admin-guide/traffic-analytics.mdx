title: "Traffic Analytics"

---

The Traffic Analytics section gives you a comprehensive view of website visitor data — who's on the site right now, where they're coming from, what they're doing, and how performance compares over time. It's powered by Google Analytics 4, and the data is presented in a format designed for quick review from a phone.

<Note>
  Traffic Analytics requires a Google Analytics 4 property to be connected to the website. This can be set up at any time and is optional.
</Note>

## Real-Time Overview

The top section shows current visitors:

- **Active users right now** — the exact count of people currently on the website
- **Live world map** — active visitors shown as pulsing dots on a geographic map
- **Top active pages** — which pages are being viewed at this moment

## Period Filters

Switch between time periods using the filter pills:
- **Today** — current day from midnight
- **7 Days** — rolling last 7 days
- **30 Days** — rolling last 30 days
- **90 Days** — rolling last 90 days

All historical charts and tables update when you change the period.

## Key Metric Cards

Four summary cards show performance for the selected period:

| Metric | What It Measures |
|--------|-----------------|
| **Sessions** | Total number of website visits. One person visiting multiple times counts as multiple sessions. |
| **Page Views** | Total number of individual pages loaded. One session often includes multiple page views. |
| **Users** | Unique visitors in the period. Multiple visits from the same person count as one user. |
| **Bounce Rate** | Percentage of sessions where the visitor left after viewing only one page. A lower bounce rate generally indicates more engaged visitors. |

## Sessions Chart

The sessions chart shows traffic volume over the selected period as a line graph. Use it to:
- Identify which days had the most traffic
- Spot the impact of a marketing campaign or announcement
- Understand whether traffic is growing, flat, or declining over time

A spike on a specific date usually corresponds to a campaign send, a viral post, or coverage in an external publication.

## Traffic Sources

Where visitors came from, broken down by source type:

| Source | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| **Direct** | Visitors who typed the URL directly or used a bookmark |
| **Organic Search** | Visitors who found the site through Google, Bing, or another search engine |
| **Social** | Visitors referred from social media platforms (Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, etc.) |
| **Referral** | Visitors from links on other websites |
| **Email** | Visitors who clicked a link in an email campaign |

Understanding traffic sources helps you see where your marketing efforts are paying off.

## Top Pages

A ranked list of the most-visited pages in the selected period, with session counts. This shows you:
- Which content is attracting the most attention
- Which product or service pages are generating the most interest
- Whether your homepage is the primary entry point or whether users are landing on specific pages

## Countries Table

All countries that sent visitors in the period, ranked by session count. Each row includes:
- Country name and flag
- Session count
- Percentage of total traffic

## Devices Donut Chart

A visual breakdown of the devices visitors used:
- **Desktop** (percentage and count)
- **Mobile** (percentage and count)
- **Tablet** (percentage and count)

If the majority of your users' traffic is mobile, this is important data — it means the website needs to perform well on small screens, and mobile-first features matter.
